This page started as a collection of notes I have compiled over my forty years of working with RS-232 and serial ports of all kinds from VAXes, Packet Switched Networks down to Intel based desktops.

The https://store.accuristech.com/standards/tia-tia-232-f?product_id=2594289 RS-232-C (now known as EIA-232-E) specification has been around since 1977 and is based on level +/-/+3-12 vdc signals. EIA-232-E is an asynchronous clock specification meaning that signals are sent irrespective of the clock of the remote device and consequently can drop signals by over or under-running the remote device. EIA-232's big brothers http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RS-422 [RS-422-B] (balanced) and [EIA-423-B] (unbalanced) are synchronous and require clock signals on pin 25.

See also my notes for using serial ports under Linux - TBA

RS-232 predates Universal Device Bus (USB) by about 20 years it is pretty much the standard now although it is still used for industrial automation, embedded systems, networking equipment, some legacy telecom (because of it some what long haul capability with repeaters) and scientific/medical devices.

Recommendations:

For those that support serial ports or modems connected to serial ports I highly recommend the following:

Obtain a good understanding of the http://www.arcelect.com/rs232.htm signals and how they "handshake" for http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flow_control flow control.

Purchase a "break out box" https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breakout_box, signal tester https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cable_tester and possibly a "BERT" https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bit_error_rate#Bit_error_rate_tester. Note that with the proliferation of digital and fiber circuits needing a BERT tester is almost obsolete any longer.

A serial line might by quite useful if you have to diagnose character losses or learn to configure a foreign device.

And finally when troubleshooting clock synchronous lines such X.25, SDLC, HDLC, SNA or DDCMP as I used to have to do in the 80s find yourself an HP 4952A or similar serial line protocol analyzer. These protocols today though have gone the way od the Dodo bird.
